\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Bayesian inference provides a principled framework for estimating unknown quantities from noisy observations. Conditional normalizing flows provide a way towards amortized Bayesian inference, which means making Bayesian parameter identification reusable across datasets \citep{radev2021amortized}. Trained on simulated parameter--observation pairs, BayesFlow \citep{radev2020bayesflow} combines a learned summary of the observations with an invertible transformation to approximate the parameter posterior. Once fitted, the model produces posterior samples for new observations without repeating parameter optimization. This amortization makes expressive Bayesian inference practical for repeated identification tasks. Its success motivates the question studied here: how can conditional normalizing flows be combined with temporal dynamics to infer evolving states and their uncertainty?

State estimation extends the inference target from a fixed parameter vector to a sequence of unknown states. Noisy or partial measurements constrain that sequence, while temporal dependence links uncertainty at one time to states at subsequent times. A useful estimator must therefore combine flexible conditional distributions, information from the observations, and a model of temporal evolution. Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) provide a natural starting point. In its standard form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sde-standard}
 dz_t=f(z_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(z_t,t)\,dW_t,
\end{equation}
an SDE combines a drift $f$, which describes the nominal evolution of the state $z_t$, with a diffusion $\sigma$ that scales the increments of a Wiener process $W_t$ and represents variability around it. A neural SDE parameterizes $f$ and $\sigma$ by neural networks, which can be trained in two established ways: Latent SDE and SDE-GAN.

Latent SDEs \citep{li2020scalable} represent time series through an underlying stochastic process and learn its dynamics through variational inference, coupling a generative SDE with an approximate posterior process. When the observation model uses a Gaussian decoder with learned variance, it can inherit a decoder variance-collapse problem. The estimated variance may approach zero, which makes training highly sensitive to small reconstruction errors and destabilizes optimization \citep{takahashi2018student,rybkin2021calibrated}. In addition, every increment of the posterior process is Gaussian given the current state, so a non-Gaussian conditional distribution of the next state can only arise through the nonlinearity of drift and diffusion over several steps.

SDE-GANs \citep{kidger2021gan} instead treat a neural SDE as a trajectory generator and train it against a discriminator to match observed path distributions. This framework accommodates flexible stochastic dynamics, but it requires coupled adversarial optimization and careful discriminator regularization \citep{kidger2021gan}. Moreover, it does not provide a tractable finite-interval transition likelihood, which restricts state estimation to simulation-based bootstrap filtering and rules out observation-informed proposals and likelihood evaluation between measurement times. Signature-kernel scores remove the discriminator and train the same generator with a proper scoring rule on paths \citep{issa2023sigkernel}.

In contrast to these two neural SDE methods, we seek to combine the temporal structure of neural SDEs with the explicit conditional densities and direct sampling offered by normalizing flows (NFs) \citep{dinh2014nice,rezende2015flows,dinh2017realnvp}. The flow transforms the noise within each state update while preserving tractable likelihoods and the sequential structure of neural SDEs. We call this construction NormFlowSDE. An observation-conditioned neural drift supplies a nominal state update, and an invertible conditional flow transforms Gaussian inputs into state-dependent noise. Both are trained jointly from paired state--measurement data. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the resulting estimator.

The construction also raises a mathematical question: under what conditions does the flow-based state update admit a continuous-time SDE limit? In Appendix~\ref{app:diffusion}, we prove that, with the learned coefficients fixed and under explicit regularity and moment assumptions, the trajectories of a centered inference scheme converge in distribution to the unique solution of an It\^o SDE as the time step tends to zero. 

Section~\ref{sec:experiments} evaluates NormFlowSDE as a causal amortized filter on two simulated systems (a controlled pendulum and the Lorenz--96 model) and two robot datasets (ALOHA insertion \citep{zhao2023aloha} and REASSEMBLE \citep{sliwowski2025reassemble}). In addition, we implement NormFlowSDE as a transition model in a particle filter. Both implementations are thoroughly benchmarked. First, NormFlowSDE as a direct amortized filter is compared with latent Neural Stochastic Flow (latent NSF), latent SDE, and a neural SDE trained with the signature-kernel score (SigKer SDE). Second, NormFlowSDE in a particle filter is compared with three NF-based methods: an NF-based differentiable particle filter (NF-DPF), Neural Stochastic Flow (NSF), and a flow-matching-based SDE (FlowSDE). The benchmark methods are described in detail in Section~\ref{sec:related}. 

Several other works use generative methods to model stochastic processes directly. The continuous-time flow process (CTFP) \citep{deng2020ctfp} maps a Wiener process through a time-indexed invertible transformation, which gives the observed process tractable finite-dimensional likelihoods. Continuous latent process flows \citep{deng2021clpf} apply such a transformation to a latent process driven by an SDE. The GP-VAE \citep{fortuin2020gpvae} combines Gaussian-process priors with deep encoders, and neural processes learn distributions over functions \citep{garnelo2018neural}. Diffusion and flow-matching models have also been applied directly to time series. TSDiff \citep{kollovieh2023tsdiff} trains an unconditional diffusion model over whole windows and obtains forecasts by guiding it toward the observed past at sampling time. TSFlow \citep{kollovieh2025tsflow} uses conditional flow matching with Gaussian-process priors to generate and forecast series. Stochastic process diffusion generates whole series as continuous functions \citep{bilos2023stochastic}. All of these models target the distribution of the entire time series and do not model a process in physical time. They therefore provide neither a transition model for a recursive filter nor a causal state estimator comparable with the methods in our work, and we do not include them in the benchmark.
